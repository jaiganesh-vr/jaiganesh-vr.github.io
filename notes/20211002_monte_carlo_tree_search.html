Monte Carlo Tree Search

Artificial Intelligence Games

Oct 02, 2021

Alpha-beta search depends on a good evaluation function. For games like Go, writing one by hand proved extremely difficult, and the branching factor of around 250 made deep search impossible. Monte Carlo tree search took a different approach: estimate the value of a position by playing many games from it.

The Idea

The value of a state is estimated as the average outcome of many simulated games, called playouts or rollouts, that start from it. Moves are chosen randomly or by a cheap policy during playouts.

Rather than spreading simulations evenly, MCTS builds a search tree asymmetrically, focusing on the most promising moves.

The Four Phases

Each iteration has four steps:

  • Selection: starting at the root, follow a selection policy down the tree until reaching a node with unexplored children.
  • Expansion: add one or more child nodes.
  • Simulation: play out a game from the new node to the end.
  • Back-propagation: update the win and visit counts of every node on the path with the result.

After many iterations, the move from the root with the most visits is played.

Exploration vs Exploitation

Selection must balance two goals: exploit moves that have won often, and explore moves that have been tried rarely and might be better than they look. The standard choice is the UCB1 formula, applied to trees as UCT:

UCB1(n) = W(n)/N(n) + C · √( ln N(parent) / N(n) )

  • W(n)/N(n) is the win rate, the exploitation term.
  • The square root term grows for rarely visited nodes, the exploration term.
  • C balances the two; √2 is a common theoretical value.

MCTS spends its simulations where the answer is most uncertain and most important.

Strengths

  • No evaluation function is needed, only the rules of the game.
  • It is an anytime algorithm: stop it whenever time runs out and use the current best move.
  • It handles large branching factors better than alpha-beta.

With Learning

AlphaGo and its successors combined MCTS with deep neural networks: a policy network guides which moves to explore, and a value network replaces or supplements random playouts. Trained by self-play, this combination reached superhuman strength in Go, chess and shogi.

Closing Thought

MCTS replaced hand-crafted knowledge with statistics. Its success, especially combined with learned networks, marked a turning point in how AI approaches complex decision problems.
