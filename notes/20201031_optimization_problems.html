Optimization Problems in AI

Artificial Intelligence Optimization

Oct 31, 2020

Search problems ask for a path to a goal. Optimization problems ask for the best state according to an objective function. Many real AI tasks, from scheduling to training a neural network, are optimization problems at heart.

The Components

  • Variables: what we can choose.
  • Objective function: what we want to maximise or minimise.
  • Constraints: conditions any solution must satisfy.

For example, placing three airports to minimise the total squared distance from each city to its nearest airport: the variables are six coordinates, and the objective is the sum of squared distances.

Local and Global Optima

A local optimum is better than all its neighbours. A global optimum is better than every state. Most practical algorithms only guarantee local optima, which is why the shape of the landscape matters so much.

Convex problems have no bad local optima. Every local minimum is the global one.

Convex problems, such as linear and quadratic programming, can be solved efficiently and reliably. Most interesting AI problems are not convex.

Continuous Spaces

When variables are continuous, there are infinitely many neighbours, so hill climbing in its basic form does not apply. Two common approaches:

  • Discretise: move each variable by a small fixed step and treat it as a discrete problem.
  • Use the gradient: the gradient ∇f points in the direction of steepest increase, so move along it.

Gradient ascent updates:

x ← x + α ∇f(x)

where α is the step size. Too small and progress is slow; too large and the search overshoots. Newton's method also uses second derivatives to choose steps more intelligently, at higher cost per step.

Constrained Optimization

Constraints restrict the feasible region. Linear programming handles linear objectives with linear constraints, and is one of the most widely applied optimization tools in industry. Lagrange multipliers turn equality-constrained problems into unconstrained ones.

Combinatorial Optimization

Many problems have discrete variables and a huge number of combinations: travelling salesperson, scheduling, packing. Exact methods exist but often take exponential time, so local search, simulated annealing and genetic algorithms are used to find good, if not provably best, solutions.

Closing Thought

Optimization is the common thread between classic AI search and modern machine learning. Training a model is, in the end, a very large optimization problem solved by following gradients.
