Uncertainty and Probability

Artificial Intelligence Probability

Jan 13, 2024

Logical agents assume facts are definitely true or false. Real agents rarely have that luxury. Sensors are noisy, the world is partially observable, and rules have exceptions. Probability theory gives agents a principled way to reason with degrees of belief.

Why Logic Falls Short

Consider diagnosing a toothache with a rule like Toothache ⇒ Cavity. It is wrong: toothaches can be caused by gum disease, abscesses and many other things. Listing every cause is impractical, and even then we would not know which applies to a particular patient.

Probability summarises that uncertainty instead of ignoring it.

Probability is a way of saying what we believe, and how strongly, given what we know.

Random Variables

A random variable represents some aspect of the world with a set of possible values. Cavity might be true or false; Weather might be sunny, rain, cloudy or snow. Each assignment of values to all variables is a possible world.

The Axioms

  • Every probability is between 0 and 1.
  • The probabilities of all possible worlds sum to 1.
  • P(a ∨ b) = P(a) + P(b) − P(a ∧ b).

An agent whose beliefs violate these axioms can be offered a set of bets it will accept and is guaranteed to lose, a result known as the Dutch book argument.

Conditional Probability

The probability of a given b:

P(a | b) = P(a ∧ b) / P(b)

P(cavity | toothache) is the probability of a cavity once we know the patient has a toothache. Rearranged, this gives the product rule: P(a ∧ b) = P(a | b) · P(b).

The Full Joint Distribution

The full joint distribution assigns a probability to every possible world. Any question can be answered from it by summing the relevant entries, a process called marginalisation.

The problem is size: with n Boolean variables, the table has 2ⁿ entries. For realistic problems this is impossible to store or estimate.

Independence

Two variables are independent if knowing one tells you nothing about the other: P(a | b) = P(a). Weather and dental problems are independent, so their joint distribution factors into two smaller tables.

Conditional independence is weaker and far more common: two variables become independent once a third is known. Toothache and the dentist's probe catching are related, but independent given whether there is a cavity.

Closing Thought

Independence and conditional independence are what make probabilistic reasoning tractable. They are the key to Bayes' rule, naive Bayes and Bayesian networks, the subjects of the next notes.
