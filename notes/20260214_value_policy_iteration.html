Value Iteration and Policy Iteration

Artificial Intelligence Decisions

Feb 14, 2026

When the transition model and rewards of an MDP are known, the optimal policy can be computed by dynamic programming. The two classic algorithms, value iteration and policy iteration, attack the Bellman equation from different directions.

Value Iteration

The Bellman equations for all states form a system of nonlinear equations, because of the max. Value iteration solves them iteratively:

  • Start with arbitrary utilities, for example all zero.
  • Update every state using the Bellman equation with the current estimates.
  • Repeat until the utilities stop changing.
def value_iteration(states, actions, P, R, gamma, eps=1e-6):
    U = {s: 0.0 for s in states}
    while True:
        delta = 0.0
        for s in states:
            best = max(
                (sum(p * (R(s, a, s2) + gamma * U[s2]) for s2, p in P(s, a))
                 for a in actions(s)),
                default=0.0)
            delta = max(delta, abs(best - U[s]))
            U[s] = best
        if delta < eps:
            return U

Why It Converges

The Bellman update is a contraction: each application brings any two utility estimates closer together by at least a factor of γ. So value iteration converges to the unique solution from any starting point, and the error shrinks geometrically.

Every sweep pulls the estimates toward the true utilities by a factor of γ.

The optimal policy often stabilises long before the utilities finish converging, which motivates policy iteration.

Policy Iteration

Policy iteration alternates two steps:

  • Policy evaluation: compute the utility of every state under the current policy.
  • Policy improvement: in every state, switch to the action that is best according to those utilities.

It stops when the improvement step changes nothing; the policy is then optimal.

Policy evaluation is easier than solving the full Bellman equation, because with a fixed policy there is no max. The equations are linear and can be solved exactly in O(n³) for n states, or approximately with a few iterations of simple updates.

Comparing the Two

  • Value iteration: cheap iterations, but may need many of them.
  • Policy iteration: expensive iterations, but typically converges in very few.
  • Modified policy iteration: evaluates each policy only approximately, often giving the best of both.

The Limits

Both algorithms require a known model and touch every state in every sweep. For large or unknown environments, neither is feasible directly. Asynchronous updates, which focus on important states, help with scale. Learning from experience, without a model, is the job of reinforcement learning.

Closing Thought

Value and policy iteration show that optimal behavior in an MDP can be computed exactly, given enough time and a perfect model. Reinforcement learning keeps their ideas while dropping both of those assumptions.
