First-Order Logic

Artificial Intelligence Logic

Jan 14, 2023

Propositional logic treats the world as a collection of facts. First- order logic sees a world of objects with properties and relations between them. That shift makes it possible to state general rules concisely, such as all humans are mortal.

The Building Blocks

  • Constants: name specific objects, like John or Paris.
  • Predicates: express properties and relations, like Brother(John, Richard) or Student(Ann).
  • Functions: map objects to objects, like FatherOf(John).
  • Variables: stand for objects, like x and y.

A term refers to an object. An atomic sentence applies a predicate to terms and is true or false. Complex sentences use the same connectives as propositional logic.

Quantifiers

Quantifiers are what give first-order logic its expressive power:

  • Universal, ∀: ∀x King(x) ⇒ Person(x), "every king is a person".
  • Existential, ∃: ∃x Crown(x) ∧ OnHead(x, John), "John has a crown on his head".

A common mistake is mixing up the connectives. With ∀, the natural connective is ⇒. With ∃, it is ∧.

∀x King(x) ∧ Person(x) says everything is a king and a person, which is not what was meant.

Nested Quantifiers and Order

The order of quantifiers changes meaning:

  • ∀x ∃y Loves(x, y): everybody loves somebody.
  • ∃y ∀x Loves(x, y): there is someone whom everybody loves.

Quantifiers are also connected through negation: ¬∀x P(x) is equivalent to ∃x ¬P(x).

Equality

The equality symbol states that two terms refer to the same object, for example Father(John) = Henry. It is needed to say things like "Richard has at least two brothers", which requires stating that the two brothers are different objects.

Knowledge Engineering

Building a knowledge base in first-order logic involves:

  • Identifying the task and the questions it must answer.
  • Choosing a vocabulary of predicates, functions and constants.
  • Encoding general knowledge as axioms.
  • Encoding the specific problem instance.
  • Posing queries and debugging the knowledge base.

Choosing the vocabulary well, called the ontology, largely determines how easy the rest is.

Closing Thought

First-order logic is expressive enough to describe much of mathematics and a great deal of common knowledge. That expressiveness comes at a cost: inference becomes semi-decidable, as the next note explains.
