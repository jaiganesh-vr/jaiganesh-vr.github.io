Bayes' Rule

Artificial Intelligence Probability

Apr 06, 2024

Bayes' rule is a short equation with an outsized role in AI. It tells an agent how to update its beliefs when new evidence arrives, and it lets us compute the probabilities we want from the ones we actually know.

The Rule

From the product rule, P(a ∧ b) = P(a | b) P(b) = P(b | a) P(a). Dividing by P(b) gives:

P(a | b) = P(b | a) · P(a) / P(b)

In the language of diagnosis, with a cause and an effect:

P(cause | effect) = P(effect | cause) · P(cause) / P(effect)

  • P(cause): the prior, belief before seeing evidence.
  • P(effect | cause): the likelihood, how expected the evidence is if the cause is present.
  • P(cause | effect): the posterior, belief after seeing evidence.

Why It Is Useful

Causal knowledge, P(effect | cause), tends to be stable and easy to obtain. Doctors know how often meningitis causes a stiff neck. Diagnostic knowledge, P(cause | effect), is what we need, but it changes with circumstances such as an epidemic.

Bayes' rule computes the diagnostic direction from the causal one. With P(stiff neck | meningitis) = 0.7, P(meningitis) = 1/50,000 and P(stiff neck) = 0.01, we get P(meningitis | stiff neck) = 0.0014. Even with a stiff neck, meningitis remains unlikely, because it is rare.

The Base Rate Trap

Suppose a disease affects 1 percent of people, and a test detects it 99 percent of the time but gives false positives 5 percent of the time. After a positive test:

P(disease | positive) = 0.99 × 0.01 / (0.99 × 0.01 + 0.05 × 0.99) ≈ 0.17

Only about 17 percent. Most positive results come from the large healthy population. Ignoring the prior, called base rate neglect, is one of the most common reasoning errors people make.

Strong evidence for a rare event can still leave it unlikely.

Normalisation

Often P(effect) is hard to obtain directly. It can be avoided: compute P(effect | cause) P(cause) for every possible cause, then divide by their sum so the results add to 1. This normalisation constant is written α.

Combining Evidence

With several pieces of evidence, Bayes' rule needs their joint likelihood, which quickly becomes too large to estimate. If the pieces of evidence are conditionally independent given the cause, the likelihood factors into a product of individual terms. That assumption leads directly to the naive Bayes model.

Closing Thought

Bayes' rule is the mathematics of learning from evidence. Much of probabilistic AI, from spam filters to robot localisation, is this equation applied carefully and at scale.
