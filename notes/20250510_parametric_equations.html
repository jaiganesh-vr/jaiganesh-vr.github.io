Parametric Equations

Mathematics Calculus

May 10, 2025

A graph y = f(x) cannot describe a full circle, since it gives only one y for each x. Writing x and y separately as functions of a third variable removes that limit, and also captures something a graph cannot: how a point moves along the curve over time.

The Idea

A parametric curve gives both coordinates in terms of a parameter t:

x = x(t), y = y(t)

The standard example is the unit circle, where each value of t gives a point, and as t runs from 0 to 2π, it goes once around anticlockwise:

x = cos t, y = sin t

You can often eliminate t to get an equation in x and y. Here, cos² t + sin² t = 1 gives x² + y² = 1. This recovers the shape but loses the motion. The same circle comes from x = sin 2t, y = cos 2t, which runs twice as fast, clockwise, from a different starting point.

The equation says where the curve is. The parametrisation also says how it gets traversed.

Slope

By the chain rule, dy/dt = (dy/dx)(dx/dt). So if dx/dt is not zero:

dy/dx = (dy/dt) / (dx/dt)

For the circle, dy/dx = cos t / (−sin t) = −cot t. Tangents are horizontal when dy/dt = 0 and vertical when dx/dt = 0. If both are zero, the point may be a cusp.

The second derivative needs care, since dy/dx itself is a function of t:

d²y/dx² = [ d/dt (dy/dx) ] / (dx/dt)

Dividing d²y/dt² by d²x/dt² is a common error. It does not give this quantity.

Arc Length

Over a small time dt, the point moves by dx and dy, so the distance moved comes from Pythagoras:

ds = √( (dx/dt)² + (dy/dt)² ) dt

The speed of the point is the factor in front of dt, and the total length is its integral:

L = ∫ √( x′(t)² + y′(t)² ) dt

For the circle of radius a, the speed is a, so the length around it is 2πa. As expected, the length does not depend on how the curve is parametrised, even though the speed does.

Area

The usual integral ∫ y dx applies with both parts converted to t:

A = ∫ y(t) x′(t) dt

The Cycloid

A point on the rim of a rolling wheel traces a cycloid. A wheel of radius a that has rotated through angle t has moved its centre by at, while the point has rotated around the centre. Adding the two motions gives the following:

x = a (t − sin t), y = a (1 − cos t)

This curve is hard to write as y = f(x), but easy to work with parametrically. Over one arch, from t = 0 to 2π:

x′² + y′² = a² ( (1 − cos t)² + sin² t ) = 2a² (1 − cos t) = 4a² sin²(t/2)

The speed is 2a |sin(t/2)|. It is zero when the point touches the ground, which explains the cusps, and greatest at the top, where it moves at twice the speed of the wheel's centre. Integrating gives two neat results:

L = 8a and A = 3πa²

So an arch has length 8 times the radius, with an area of 3 times the area of the wheel. 

import numpy as np

t = np.linspace(0, 2 * np.pi, 1_000_001)
x = t - np.sin(t)      # cycloid, a = 1
y = 1 - np.cos(t)

length = np.hypot(np.diff(x), np.diff(y)).sum()
area = ((y[1:] + y[:-1]) / 2 * np.diff(x)).sum()

print(round(length, 6))              # 8.0
print(round(area, 6), round(3 * np.pi, 6))

The cycloid also has a famous dynamic property. It is the brachistochrone, the curve down which a bead slides from one point to another the fastest under gravity. It beats the straight line.

Polar Curves

Polar coordinates are a special case, with θ as the parameter: x = r(θ) cos θ and y = r(θ) sin θ. The general formulas become ds = √(r² + r′²) dθ for the arc length, and A = ½ ∫ r² dθ for the area swept from the origin.

In Higher Dimensions

Adding a third coordinate z(t) gives a space curve, such as the helix (cos t, sin t, t). Its velocity vector (x′, y′, z′) is tangent to the curve. Bézier curves in graphic design and font outlines are also parametric, and they are used for the same reason: a parametric curve can loop, turn and double back without trouble.

Closing Thought

A parameter gives us a free variable to do the bookkeeping. It separates the path from the speed along it, and lets curves that are not functions be handled by ordinary calculus. Another case where direct evaluation fails is a limit in which two competing quantities both vanish.
