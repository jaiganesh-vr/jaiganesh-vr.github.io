The Exponential Family

Mathematics Probability

Apr 11, 2026

The Bernoulli, Gaussian, Poisson, categorical, gamma and Beta distributions appear to be unrelated. They are all members of one family, and the properties that make them convenient, such as simple maximum likelihood and conjugate priors, come from this shared form.

The Form

A distribution belongs to the exponential family if it can be written as:

p(x | η) = h(x) · exp( ηᵀ T(x) − A(η) )

  • Natural parameters, η: a reparameterised version of the usual parameters.
  • Sufficient statistics, T(x): the only functions of the data that the likelihood uses.
  • Log-partition function, A(η): the normaliser, which makes the distribution integrate to one.
  • Base measure, h(x): a term that does not depend on the parameters.

Example: The Bernoulli

Take p(x | μ) = μˣ (1 − μ)¹⁻ˣ and rewrite it as an exponential of a logarithm:

p(x) = exp( x · log(μ / (1 − μ)) + log(1 − μ) )

This gives T(x) = x and a natural parameter that is the familiar log-odds:

η = log( μ / (1 − μ) )

Inverting it gives μ = 1 / (1 + e^(−η)), the sigmoid. The sigmoid of logistic regression is simply the map from a natural parameter back to a mean. Similarly, softmax is the map for the categorical distribution.

Moments From A

A remarkable fact is that derivatives of A give the moments of the sufficient statistics:

∇A(η) = E[T(x)] and ∇²A(η) = Cov[T(x)]

Since a covariance matrix is positive semi-definite, A must be convex. For the Bernoulli, A(η) = log(1 + e^η). Its derivative is the sigmoid, which is the mean. Its second derivative is μ(1 − μ), which is the variance.

Normally, computing moments means integrating. In this family it means differentiating. 

import numpy as np

def A(eta):
    return np.log(1 + np.exp(eta))     # Bernoulli log-partition

eta, h = 0.8, 1e-4
mean = (A(eta + h) - A(eta - h)) / (2 * h)
variance = (A(eta + h) - 2 * A(eta) + A(eta - h)) / h**2

mu = 1 / (1 + np.exp(-eta))
print(round(mean, 6), round(mu, 6))               # match
print(round(variance, 6), round(mu * (1 - mu), 6))  # match

Maximum Likelihood

The log-likelihood is linear in T, minus A, and hence concave in η. The gradient gives a clean result:

∇A(η) = (1/n) Σ T(xᵢ)

The maximum likelihood estimate matches the expected sufficient statistics to their empirical averages. Fitting a Gaussian comes down to matching the mean and second moment. It also means the dataset can be reduced to a fixed-size summary, regardless of n, which is why streaming updates are so easy for these models. These distributions are also the exact ones in which a fixed-size sufficient statistic exists, under mild conditions.

Conjugate Priors

Every member has a conjugate prior. Its form mirrors the likelihood:

p(η | τ, ν) ∝ exp( ηᵀτ − ν A(η) )

Updating involves adding sufficient statistics to τ and the count n to ν. This is the general pattern behind Beta-Bernoulli, Dirichlet-categorical and Gaussian-Gaussian updates. Priors behave like pseudo-observations.

Generalised Linear Models

A GLM makes the natural parameter a linear function of the input, η = wᵀx, and picks the distribution that fits the target's type:

  • Gaussian: real-valued targets, giving linear regression.
  • Bernoulli: binary targets, giving logistic regression.
  • Categorical: multi-class targets, giving softmax regression.
  • Poisson: count targets, giving Poisson regression.

The gradient has the same form for all of them, Σ (E[y | xᵢ] − yᵢ) xᵢ, and the negative log-likelihood is always convex in w. Their shared properties come straight from this family. A neural network with one of these output layers is a GLM on learned features.

The Maximum Entropy View

Among all distributions that satisfy given constraints on E[T(x)], the one with maximum entropy lies in the exponential family with those sufficient statistics. Fix mean and variance on the real line and you get the Gaussian. This explains in what sense these distributions make the fewest assumptions beyond what was specified.

Limits

Not everything fits. Mixtures, the Student t and any distribution whose support depends on its parameters, such as the uniform on [0, θ], are not members. And models built with latent variables lose the nice properties, which is what EM and variational methods are for.

Closing Thought

A long list of distributions becomes one idea with a few variations. Once you can see the common form, loss functions, link functions and conjugate updates stop being separate facts to memorise.
