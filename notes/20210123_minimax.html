Adversarial Search and Minimax

Artificial Intelligence Games

Jan 23, 2021

In ordinary search, the agent controls every action. In games, an opponent chooses half the moves and is actively working against you. Adversarial search handles that by assuming the opponent plays as well as possible.

Defining a Game

A two-player game is defined by:

  • The initial state and whose turn it is.
  • The legal actions in each state.
  • A transition model and a terminal test that detects the end of the game.
  • A utility function giving the final payoff, such as +1, 0 or −1.

In zero-sum games like chess, one player's gain is exactly the other's loss.

The Game Tree

The two players are called MAX and MIN. MAX moves first and wants the highest utility; MIN wants the lowest. The game tree alternates MAX and MIN levels, with utilities at the leaves.

Minimax

The minimax value of a node is the utility of the outcome if both players play optimally from that point:

  • At a terminal node, its utility.
  • At a MAX node, the maximum of its children's values.
  • At a MIN node, the minimum of its children's values.
def minimax(state, maximizing):
    if state.is_terminal():
        return state.utility()
    values = (minimax(child, not maximizing) for child in state.children())
    return max(values) if maximizing else min(values)

MAX picks the move leading to the child with the highest minimax value.

Minimax assumes the worst case: the opponent will always make the move that is best for them.

Complexity

Minimax explores the full game tree, so its time is O(b^m) for branching factor b and maximum depth m. Chess has an average branching factor of about 35 and games of around 80 plies, which makes full search impossible.

Imperfect Real-Time Decisions

Real programs stop the search at a depth limit and apply an evaluation function, an estimate of how good a position is. In chess, a classic evaluation is a weighted sum of features like material, mobility and king safety.

Cutting off search introduces the horizon effect: a bad outcome just beyond the depth limit is invisible, so a program may make delaying moves that only postpone it.

Closing Thought

Minimax is a clean definition of optimal play. The practical work in game-playing AI is about approximating it: searching less of the tree, and judging positions well where the search stops.
