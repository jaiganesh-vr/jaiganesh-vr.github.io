What Is Artificial Intelligence?

Artificial Intelligence Foundations

Dec 08, 2018

Artificial intelligence is one of those terms everyone uses and few define. Before studying any algorithm, it helps to be clear about what the field is actually trying to build.

Four Definitions

Definitions of AI tend to fall along two questions: is the goal about thinking or about behaving, and is it measured against humans or against an ideal standard of correctness? That gives four views:

  • Thinking humanly: model how people actually think, the cognitive science approach.
  • Acting humanly: behave so that people cannot tell the difference, the idea behind the Turing test.
  • Thinking rationally: reason correctly using logic, the laws of thought approach.
  • Acting rationally: do the right thing, given what you know, to achieve your goals.

Why Acting Rationally Won

Most of modern AI adopts the last view. A rational agent is one that acts to achieve the best expected outcome, given what it perceives and what it knows.

Rationality is a standard you can define precisely. Human-likeness is not.

Acting rationally is more general than thinking rationally, because correct inference is only one way to act well. Sometimes a reflex is the rational choice: pulling a hand away from a hot stove does not need a proof. It is also easier to build and test than human imitation, because the target can be stated mathematically.

The Foundations

AI draws on many older disciplines:

  • Philosophy: logic, reasoning and the question of what a mind is.
  • Mathematics: formal logic, computation and probability.
  • Economics: utility and decision-making under uncertainty.
  • Neuroscience and psychology: how biological minds actually work.
  • Computer engineering: the machines that make any of it possible.

A Note on Limits

Perfect rationality is usually impossible: the computation needed to always find the best action is too expensive in complex environments. Much of AI is therefore about bounded rationality, acting as well as possible with limited time and computation.

Closing Thought

Framing AI as building rational agents turns a philosophical debate into an engineering problem. The rest of these notes follow from that frame: search, logic and probability are all tools for choosing good actions.
