Maximum Likelihood and MAP Estimation

Machine Learning Estimation

Aug 21, 2021

A full posterior is often too expensive to compute or use. Point estimation picks one value of θ instead. The two standard choices differ only in whether they use a prior.

Maximum Likelihood

Maximum likelihood estimation chooses the parameters that make the observed data most probable. Assuming independent examples, and taking logs because sums are easier to work with than products:

θ_MLE = argmax Σ log P(xᵢ | θ)

For a Gaussian, setting the gradient to zero gives two familiar answers:

μ = (1/n) Σ xᵢ and σ² = (1/n) Σ (xᵢ − μ)²

For 7 heads in 10 coin flips it gives θ = 0.7.

MAP Estimation

Maximum a posteriori estimation chooses the peak of the posterior. The evidence does not depend on θ, so it can be dropped:

θ_MAP = argmax [ Σ log P(xᵢ | θ) + log P(θ) ]

With a Beta(a, b) prior on a coin with k heads in n flips:

θ_MAP = (k + a − 1) / (n + a + b − 2)

A Beta(2, 2) prior on our coin gives 8 / 12 ≈ 0.667. With a uniform prior, Beta(1, 1), MAP and maximum likelihood coincide.

MAP is maximum likelihood plus a penalty, and the penalty is the log prior.

Losses Are Likelihoods

Many everyday loss functions are negative log-likelihoods under some noise model:

  • Squared error: targets with Gaussian noise around the prediction.
  • Absolute error: targets with Laplace noise, which is why it is more tolerant of outliers.
  • Cross-entropy: targets drawn from a Bernoulli or categorical distribution given by the model.

Priors become regularisers in the same way. A Gaussian prior on weights gives an L2 penalty, and a Laplace prior gives an L1 penalty.

A Failure of Maximum Likelihood

With little data, maximum likelihood overfits. If a coin lands heads three times out of three, the estimate is θ = 1, which declares tails impossible. A word never seen in a class gets probability zero in the same way. Priors fix this by keeping some probability on everything, which is why smoothing is really MAP or posterior mean estimation.

What Point Estimates Lose

  • No uncertainty: 7 heads in 10 flips and 7,000 heads in 10,000 flips give the same estimate, even though we should trust them very differently.
  • The peak can be unrepresentative: the mode of a skewed posterior may sit far from where most of its probability lies.
  • MAP depends on parameterisation: rewriting θ as a different variable, such as its logarithm, can move the peak. Maximum likelihood does not have this problem.

Closing Thought

Maximum likelihood trusts the data alone, and MAP adds a modest amount of prior belief. Both throw away the rest of the distribution, and sometimes what is thrown away is what we need, as with comparing models of different complexity.
