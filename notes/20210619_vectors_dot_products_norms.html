Vectors, Dot Products and Norms

Mathematics Linear Algebra

Jun 19, 2021

Almost everything in machine learning is eventually a list of numbers: an image, a word embedding, the weights of a model. Treating those lists as vectors lets us use geometry to reason about them.

What a Vector Is

A vector in ℝⁿ is an ordered list of n numbers. It can be read in two useful ways: as a point in space, or as an arrow from the origin, with a direction and a length. Vectors add component by component, and scaling multiplies every component.

A combination of the two gives the most important operation in linear algebra:

a₁v₁ + a₂v₂ + … + aₖvₖ

This is a linear combination. The set of everything reachable this way is called the span. Vectors are linearly independent if none of them can be written as a combination of the others, that is, if each adds a truly new direction.

The Dot Product

Multiply matching components and add:

xᵀy = Σ xᵢyᵢ

This algebraic definition has an equally important geometric form:

xᵀy = ‖x‖ ‖y‖ cos θ

So the dot product measures alignment. It is positive when two vectors point in a similar direction, zero when they are perpendicular, and negative when they point in opposite directions.

A linear model's score, wᵀx, is just a measure of how aligned the input is with the weights.

Dividing out the lengths gives cosine similarity, which is how embeddings are commonly compared.

Projections

The dot product also finds how much of one vector lies along another. The projection of x onto y is:

proj(x) = ( xᵀy / yᵀy ) · y

The remaining part, x − proj(x), is perpendicular to y. Least squares regression is the same idea, since it projects the target onto the space spanned by the features.

Norms

A norm measures the size of a vector. It must be zero only for the zero vector, scale with the vector, and obey the triangle inequality. The Lp norms form a common family:

‖x‖ₚ = ( Σ |xᵢ|ᵖ )^(1/p)

  • L2 norm: ordinary Euclidean length, √(Σ xᵢ²).
  • L1 norm: the sum of absolute values, also known as Manhattan distance.
  • L∞ norm: the largest absolute component.

For x = (3, −4), these give 5, 7 and 4 respectively. 

import numpy as np

x = np.array([3.0, -4.0])
y = np.array([4.0, 3.0])

print(x @ y)                         # 0.0: perpendicular
print(np.linalg.norm(x))             # 5.0
print(np.linalg.norm(x, 1))          # 7.0
print(np.linalg.norm(x, np.inf))     # 4.0

Why Different Norms Matter

Norms determine how penalties behave in machine learning. The set of points with L2 norm at most 1 is a disc, and its smooth boundary leads to weights that shrink evenly. The set with L1 norm at most 1 is a diamond whose corners sit on the axes. A solution constrained to it often lands on a corner, where some components are exactly zero, which is why L1 regularisation gives sparse models.

The Cauchy-Schwarz Inequality

A short and useful fact follows from the geometric form, since |cos θ| ≤ 1:

|xᵀy| ≤ ‖x‖ ‖y‖

It is why cosine similarity always stays between −1 and 1, and it shows up in many proofs about bounds and convergence.

Closing Thought

Vectors let us turn questions about data into questions about direction and distance. Next come the objects that act on them, since a matrix is best understood as a transformation of space.
