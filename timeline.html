AI Timeline

Language models and agents 2017 to today

The Transformer turns language into the field's centre of gravity. Scale keeps paying off, a chat interface reaches hundreds of millions of people, and attention shifts from what models know to what they can do.

2024
Model Context Protocol

An open standard for connecting models to tools, as agents go mainstream.

→ The Model Context Protocol, Explained
2024
The Nobel Prizes go to AI

Hopfield and Hinton in physics, and Hassabis and Jumper in chemistry for AlphaFold.

2024
Reasoning models

Models start spending more compute at test time to think before answering.

→ Reasoning Models and Test-Time Compute
2023
GPT-4 and Llama

Multimodal frontier models, and capable open-weight models, arrive in the same year.

→ The Llama 3 Herd of Models
2022
ChatGPT

A chat interface and RLHF put language models in front of everyone.

→ From RLHF to DPO
2020
Retrieval-Augmented Generation

The RAG paper connects language models to outside knowledge.

→ Retrieval-Augmented Generation
2020
GPT-3

Scale alone produces surprisingly general ability.

→ Scaling Laws, Briefly
2018
BERT and GPT-1

Pretraining on huge amounts of text becomes the standard recipe.

2017
Attention Is All You Need

The Transformer: the architecture behind today's language models.

→ The Math Inside Attention
The deep learning boom 2012 to 2016

In 2012 a neural network wins ImageNet by a wide margin, and the threads finally meet: old ideas, GPUs and big data. Within a few years, deep learning dominates vision, speech, games and language.

2016
AlphaGo beats Lee Sedol

Deep networks plus tree search crack a game long thought out of reach.

→ Monte Carlo Tree Search
2015
ResNet

Residual connections make very deep networks trainable.

→ Layer Normalization and Residual Connections
2014
GANs and attention

Goodfellow's adversarial networks, and Bahdanau's attention for translation, both appear.

2013
Deep Q-Networks

A single network learns to play Atari games from raw pixels.

→ Deep Q-Networks
2013
word2vec

Words become vectors whose geometry captures meaning.

→ Embedding Models
2012
AlexNet

GPU training, big data and old ideas finally meet. Deep learning takes over.

The quiet build-up 1993 to 2011

"AI" is out of fashion and most researchers call their work machine learning. Practical methods spread, the web produces data at a new scale, and graphics chips become programmable. Nobody yet knows these pieces belong together.

2011
Watson wins Jeopardy!

IBM's system beats the champions in a public demonstration of language processing.

2009
ImageNet

A labelled dataset large enough to show what big models could do.

2007
CUDA

Nvidia makes graphics chips programmable for general computing.

2006
Deep belief networks

Hinton shows deep networks can be trained layer by layer, and the term deep learning catches on.

1998
LeNet-5

LeCun's convolutional network reads bank cheques in real deployments.

1997
Deep Blue beats Kasparov

Search and special hardware win at chess, without any learning from scratch.

→ Alpha-Beta Pruning
1997
LSTM

Hochreiter and Schmidhuber give networks a longer memory, laying groundwork for language models.

→ Long Short Term Memory Networks
1995
Support vector machines

Cortes and Vapnik publish the soft-margin SVM, which leads machine learning for over a decade.

→ Support Vector Machines
The second AI winter 1987 to 1993

Expert systems prove brittle and expensive, and specialised AI hardware loses out to cheaper desktops. Under the gloom, the methods that will replace hand-written rules are maturing: probability, reinforcement learning and convolutional networks.

1992
TD-Gammon

A network teaches itself backgammon to near-expert level through self-play.

1989
The World Wide Web proposed

Berners-Lee's proposal starts what becomes the largest dataset ever assembled.

1989
Convolutional networks read digits

LeCun trains a network with backpropagation to read handwritten zip codes.

1989
Q-learning

Watkins shows an agent can learn good behaviour from rewards alone.

→ Q-Learning
1988
Probabilistic reasoning

Judea Pearl's book makes Bayesian networks a foundation for reasoning under uncertainty.

→ Bayesian Networks
1987
The expert-systems market collapses

The Lisp machine market falls apart and funding dries up.

Expert systems and new networks 1980 to 1986

AI returns as a business. Companies pay to capture human expertise as rules, and national programmes compete. At the same time, neural networks come back through Hopfield's work and backpropagation.

1986
Backpropagation popularised

Rumelhart, Hinton and Williams show multi-layer networks can learn useful representations.

→ Backpropagation Is Just the Chain Rule
1982
Hopfield networks

A physicist makes neural networks respectable again.

1982
Japan's Fifth Generation project

A national AI programme prompts investment around the world.

1980
XCON at DEC

An expert system configures computer orders and saves real money, starting a commercial wave.

The first AI winter 1973 to 1980

Programs that shone on toy problems collapse on real ones. Computers are too slow, knowledge is too hard to write down, and funders lose patience. The work continues quietly, including a backpropagation idea that few people read.

1975
MYCIN

A rule-based system at Stanford diagnoses blood infections, pointing the way to expert systems.

1974
Backpropagation in a thesis

Paul Werbos describes training multi-layer networks. It goes largely unnoticed.

1973
The Lighthill report

A critical UK review helps bring on the first AI winter.

The golden years 1956 to 1972

A small group at Dartmouth names the field and believes its core problems will fall within a generation. Early programs look astonishing, funding pours in, and the hard limits stay out of sight.

1971
The microprocessor

The Intel 4004 puts a computer's processor on one chip.

1969
ARPANET

The first link of the network that becomes the internet.

1969
Perceptrons, the book

Minsky and Papert show what single-layer networks cannot do. Interest drifts away.

1966
ELIZA

A simple pattern matcher convinces people it understands them.

1965
Moore's law

Gordon Moore predicts the steady growth in transistors per chip that compute still leans on.

1958
LISP

McCarthy creates the language that shapes AI programming for decades.

1958
The perceptron

Rosenblatt builds a machine that learns from examples.

1956
The Dartmouth workshop

McCarthy, Minsky, Rochester and Shannon's summer project gives the field its name.

→ What Is Artificial Intelligence?
Foundations 1936 to 1955

Before anyone said "artificial intelligence", a few people were working out what computing even is. Turing defined what a machine could do in principle, Shannon turned information into mathematics, and electronics made the ideas physical.

1950
"Can machines think?"

Turing proposes the imitation game.

1948
Information theory

Shannon gives information a precise mathematical measure, the entropy behind today's loss functions.

→ Cross-Entropy
1947
The transistor

Bell Labs makes computing small, cheap and eventually everywhere.

1945
ENIAC and the stored-program idea

Electronic computing arrives, and von Neumann describes the architecture still in use.

1943
McCulloch-Pitts neuron

The first mathematical model of a neural network.

1936
Turing's computable numbers

A mathematical model of a machine that can carry out any procedure.
