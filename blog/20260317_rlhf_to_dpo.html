From RLHF to DPO: Teaching Models Preferences

Machine Learning Alignment

Mar 17, 2026

A pre-trained language model is an excellent autocomplete engine. Ask it a question and it may answer, or it may continue with three more questions, because that is also plausible text. Turning it into a useful assistant requires teaching it which outputs people actually prefer.

Supervised Fine-Tuning First

The first step is usually supervised fine-tuning on examples of good behavior: prompts paired with high-quality responses. This teaches the format of being an assistant.

But demonstrations only show one good answer per prompt. They do not teach the model to distinguish a good answer from a slightly worse one, and that is where preference data comes in.

RLHF

Reinforcement learning from human feedback works in three stages:

  • Collect comparisons: for each prompt, people choose the better of two model responses.
  • Train a reward model to predict which response people prefer.
  • Optimise the language model with reinforcement learning, typically PPO, to maximise that reward.

A penalty on the KL divergence from the original model keeps it from drifting too far and exploiting quirks in the reward model.

Reward models are proxies. Optimise any proxy hard enough and it stops measuring what you meant.

RLHF works, but it is complex: several models in memory at once, sensitive hyperparameters and unstable training runs.

Direct Preference Optimization

DPO observed that the reward model and RL stage can be folded into a single supervised loss. Given a preferred response and a rejected one, DPO directly increases the likelihood of the preferred response relative to the rejected one, measured against a frozen reference model.

  • No separate reward model to train.
  • No sampling during training.
  • Behavior similar to KL-regularised RLHF, with much simpler code.

That simplicity made preference tuning accessible well beyond large labs, and many variants have followed.

Feedback From Models

Human comparisons are expensive, so many pipelines use models to judge responses against written principles, with humans auditing samples. And for tasks with checkable answers, like maths or code, rewards can come from verification rather than preference at all.

Closing Thought

Preference tuning is where a model's raw capability gets shaped into behavior. Much of what people experience as a model's personality and helpfulness is decided at this stage, not in pre-training.
