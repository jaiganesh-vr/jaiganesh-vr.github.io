Synthetic Data: Promise and Pitfalls

Machine Learning Data

Jul 15, 2026

High-quality human-written data is finite, expensive to label and increasingly contested. Synthetic data, generated by models rather than collected from people, has become a central part of how modern models are trained, especially after pre-training.

Where It Works Well

  • Instruction tuning: generating diverse prompts and high-quality responses to teach assistant behavior.
  • Reasoning: producing step-by-step solutions to maths and coding problems, keeping only those that reach a verified answer.
  • Rare cases: creating examples of edge cases that are scarce in real data, such as unusual formats or languages.
  • Evaluation: building test sets for specific behaviors when real examples are hard to collect.

Synthetic data works best when something other than the generator decides what is correct.

Verification Is the Key

The most successful uses pair generation with checking. A model writes a thousand solutions to a programming problem; the ones that pass the tests become training data. A model answers maths questions; only answers that match the known result are kept.

Without verification, synthetic data simply teaches a model to imitate another model's mistakes along with its strengths.

The Pitfalls

  • Model collapse: training repeatedly on a model's own output narrows the distribution. Rare patterns disappear, and quality degrades over generations.
  • Homogeneity: generated text tends to share phrasing and structure, reducing diversity even when topics vary.
  • Inherited errors: factual mistakes and biases in the generator are copied and amplified.
  • Benchmark leakage: generators may reproduce test questions they saw in training, inflating evaluation results.

Keeping It Useful

  • Mix synthetic data with real data rather than replacing it.
  • Seed generation with diverse real examples, personas or topics to fight sameness.
  • Filter aggressively with verifiers, rubric graders and deduplication.
  • Track the provenance of every dataset so problems can be traced.

Closing Thought

Synthetic data does not create knowledge from nothing. Its value comes from cheap generation combined with reliable selection. The generator proposes, and verification decides what the next model learns.
