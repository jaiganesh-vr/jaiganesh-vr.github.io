KL Divergence Without the Fear

Mathematics Information Theory

May 10, 2026

KL divergence appears all over machine learning: in knowledge distillation, in the penalty that keeps RLHF from drifting, in variational autoencoders and in policy optimisation. It looks intimidating, but the idea is approachable.

The Definition

For two probability distributions P and Q over the same outcomes:

KL(P ‖ Q) = Σ P(x) · log( P(x) / Q(x) )

It measures the extra surprise you experience, on average, when outcomes actually come from P but you expected them according to Q.

  • It is always zero or positive.
  • It is zero only when P and Q are identical.
  • It is not a distance, because it is not symmetric.

The Link to Cross-Entropy

KL divergence is the gap between cross-entropy and entropy:

KL(P ‖ Q) = H(P, Q) − H(P)

When P is the data distribution, H(P) is fixed. So training a model Q by minimising cross-entropy is exactly the same as minimising its KL divergence from the data. Every language model's training objective is, in this sense, a KL objective.

Why Direction Matters

KL(P ‖ Q) and KL(Q ‖ P) generally differ, and the difference has practical consequences when Q is a simpler model fitting a complex P.

  • Forward KL, KL(P ‖ Q): heavily penalises Q for assigning low probability where P has mass. Q spreads out to cover every mode of P, even if that means putting mass in between. This is called mode-covering.
  • Reverse KL, KL(Q ‖ P): heavily penalises Q for putting mass where P has little. Q picks one mode and fits it tightly. This is called mode-seeking.

Forward KL says don't miss anything. Reverse KL says don't make anything up.

Where You Meet It

  • Distillation: students minimise KL from the teacher's distribution.
  • RLHF: a KL penalty keeps the tuned model close to the reference model, limiting reward hacking.
  • VAEs: a KL term keeps learned latent distributions close to a simple prior.

In each case, the direction was chosen deliberately, based on which kind of mistake is worse.

Closing Thought

KL divergence is a precise way of asking how wrong one distribution is about another. Once its asymmetry clicks, a lot of design choices in modern training stop looking arbitrary.
