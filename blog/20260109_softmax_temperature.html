Softmax and Temperature

Mathematics LLMs

Jan 09, 2026

Every time a language model produces a token, it first produces a score for every token in its vocabulary. These scores, called logits, can be any real number. Softmax is the function that turns them into a probability distribution.

Temperature is the single knob most people touch when using a model, and it acts directly on that function.

The Formula

For logits z₁ … zₙ, softmax assigns each token the probability:

p(i) = exp(zᵢ) / Σⱼ exp(zⱼ)

Exponentiation makes every value positive, and dividing by the sum makes them add up to one. Because the exponential grows quickly, softmax exaggerates differences: a token whose logit is 2 higher than another ends up roughly 7.4 times more likely.

Why Exponentials

Softmax is not an arbitrary choice. It is the natural pairing for cross-entropy loss, and its gradient has a clean form: the predicted probability minus the true label. That simplicity is a large part of why training large networks with it is stable.

It is also shift-invariant. Adding the same constant to every logit changes nothing, which is why implementations subtract the maximum logit before exponentiating. The result is identical, and it avoids overflow.

What Temperature Does

Temperature T divides the logits before softmax:

p(i) = exp(zᵢ / T) / Σⱼ exp(zⱼ / T)

  • T = 1: the model's distribution as trained.
  • T below 1: differences are amplified, so the top token dominates. As T approaches 0, sampling becomes greedy decoding.
  • T above 1: differences shrink and the distribution flattens toward uniform. Rare tokens get picked more often.

Temperature does not make a model more or less intelligent. It changes how much of the probability mass outside the top choice gets explored.

Choosing a Value

For extraction, classification and code, low temperatures tend to work well, because there is usually one right answer and variety is a liability. For brainstorming and creative writing, moderate temperatures produce more varied output without drifting into nonsense.

Very high temperatures rarely help. Once the long tail of the vocabulary gets meaningful probability, output degrades quickly, which is why temperature is usually combined with top-p or top-k truncation.

Closing Thought

Softmax is one line of math, but it sits at the exact point where a model's knowledge becomes a choice. Understanding it makes sampling settings feel less like superstition and more like engineering.
