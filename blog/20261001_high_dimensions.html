High Dimensions Are Weird

Mathematics Embeddings

Oct 01, 2026

Embedding vectors typically have hundreds or thousands of dimensions. We picture them as points on a 2D plot, but high-dimensional space behaves in ways that 2D and 3D intuition gets badly wrong. A few of those behaviors explain a lot about how embeddings and vector search work.

Almost Everything Is Orthogonal

Pick two random directions in 3D, and the angle between them could be anything. Pick two random directions in 1,000 dimensions, and they are almost certainly close to perpendicular. Their cosine similarity concentrates tightly around zero, with spread shrinking roughly as one over the square root of the dimension.

In high dimensions, there is room for an enormous number of nearly unrelated directions.

This is part of why embeddings work. A space with a thousand dimensions can hold vast numbers of distinct concepts as nearly independent directions, with little interference between them.

Volume Lives Near the Surface

In high dimensions, almost all the volume of a ball sits in a thin shell near its surface. For a 1,000-dimensional ball, more than 99.99 percent of the volume is in the outer 1 percent of the radius.

One consequence: random points drawn from common distributions in high dimensions mostly lie at nearly the same distance from the centre.

Distances Concentrate

For random points, the gap between the nearest and farthest neighbour shrinks relative to the distances themselves as dimension grows. Every point starts to look roughly equally far from every other.

This is the curse of dimensionality for search. It is why exact nearest neighbour search gets hard, and why approximate methods like HNSW are used.

Why Real Embeddings Still Work

Real embeddings are not random. Meaningful data lies near much lower-dimensional structure within the space, and related texts cluster together. Similarity search works because the signal lives in that structure, even though the full space is vast.

It also explains a practical observation: similarity scores between unrelated texts are rarely near zero for a given model, and absolute score thresholds are unreliable. Relative rankings carry the meaning.

Closing Thought

High-dimensional geometry is strange, but it is not a bug. The same properties that make distances misleading are what let embedding spaces hold so many concepts at once.
