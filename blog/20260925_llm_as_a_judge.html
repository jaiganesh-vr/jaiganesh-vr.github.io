LLM-as-a-Judge

Machine Learning Evaluation

Sep 25, 2026

Many qualities of a model's output, like helpfulness, tone, faithfulness to sources or adherence to instructions, cannot be checked with exact matching. Human review is the gold standard but slow and expensive. Using a language model as the grader has become a standard way to evaluate at scale.

How It Works

A judge model receives the task, the output to evaluate, and grading criteria, and returns a score or a verdict. Common formats include:

  • Pointwise scoring: rate one output on a scale against a rubric.
  • Pairwise comparison: decide which of two outputs is better.
  • Reference-based grading: compare an output to a known good answer.
  • Binary checks: answer specific yes or no questions, such as whether every claim is supported by the source.

Known Biases

Judges are useful but not neutral. Well-documented biases include:

  • Position bias: in pairwise comparisons, preferring whichever answer appears first, or second.
  • Verbosity bias: favouring longer answers regardless of quality.
  • Self-preference: rating outputs from the same model family more highly.
  • Surface polish: rewarding confident, well-formatted answers even when they are wrong.

A judge is a model too. Evaluate it before you trust it.

Making Judges Reliable

  • Use specific rubrics. "Does the answer cite a source for each claim?" is far more reliable than "Is this a good answer?"
  • Prefer binary or narrow scales over fine-grained 1 to 10 scores, which drift.
  • Ask for reasoning before the verdict, so the judgment is grounded.
  • Swap positions in pairwise comparisons and only count consistent verdicts.
  • Calibrate against humans: grade a sample by hand and measure agreement before relying on the judge.

Where It Fits

Judges are best for fast iteration: comparing prompt versions, catching regressions, and filtering large volumes of output. For high-stakes decisions and final evaluations, they should complement human review, not replace it.

Where correctness can be checked by code, such as running tests or validating data, code-based checks are cheaper and more reliable than any judge.

Closing Thought

LLM-as-a-judge makes it possible to evaluate the things that matter most and are hardest to measure. Its value depends entirely on the care put into its rubric and its calibration.
