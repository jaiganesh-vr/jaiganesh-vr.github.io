How Vision-Language Models See

Machine Learning Multimodal

Oct 16, 2026

Many language models now accept images: screenshots, charts, photos and scanned documents. They do not see images the way people do. An image is converted into a sequence of vectors that the language model treats much like text tokens.

From Pixels to Patches

Most vision-language models start with a vision encoder based on the Vision Transformer. The image is cut into a grid of small patches, for example 14 × 14 pixels each. Each patch is flattened into a vector, projected and given a position embedding, then processed by transformer layers.

The output is one vector per patch, each describing that region in the context of the whole image.

Aligning Images With Language

The vision encoder is often pre-trained contrastively, as in CLIP: it learns from large numbers of image and caption pairs to place matching images and texts close together in a shared space. That gives its features a meaning that relates to language.

Connecting to the Language Model

A small projection network, sometimes just a few layers, maps the vision encoder's outputs into the language model's embedding space. The resulting image tokens are inserted into the input sequence alongside text tokens.

To the language model, an image is a passage written in a learned visual vocabulary.

The combined model is then trained on image and text data, from captions to visual question answering to documents, so that the language model learns to use those tokens.

Resolution and Cost

A high-resolution image can produce hundreds or thousands of tokens, which costs context space and money. Models handle this by resizing images, tiling large images into crops, or compressing patch tokens. Small text in a downscaled screenshot can become unreadable, which is a common source of errors.

Where They Struggle

  • Precise counting and measurement: patch-level representations blur exact quantities and positions.
  • Fine spatial reasoning: questions about exact layout or relative positions remain harder than recognition.
  • Dense small text: depends heavily on resolution.
  • Charts: reading exact values from plots is error-prone without the underlying data.

These limits matter for agents that operate computers through screenshots, where precise positions are essential.

Closing Thought

Vision-language models extend the same recipe that made language models work: turn everything into tokens and let attention connect them. The images become part of the conversation, quirks and all.
