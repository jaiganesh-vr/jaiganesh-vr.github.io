Perplexity: What the Number Actually Means

Mathematics Evaluation

Jun 03, 2026

Perplexity was the headline metric for language models long before benchmarks and leaderboards took over. It still appears in papers and training dashboards, and it has an intuitive meaning once you unpack it.

The Definition

Perplexity is the exponential of the average cross-entropy loss per token:

PPL = exp( −(1/N) Σ log p(tokenᵢ | previous tokens) )

Equivalently, it is the inverse of the geometric mean probability the model assigned to each actual next token.

The Intuition

A perplexity of k means the model is, on average, as uncertain as if it were choosing uniformly among k options at each step.

  • A model that always predicts the right token with certainty has perplexity 1.
  • A model guessing uniformly over a 50,000-token vocabulary has perplexity 50,000.
  • A strong model on ordinary text may sit in the single digits.

Perplexity is the effective number of choices the model is hesitating between.

This makes it easier to read than raw loss. A drop from 2.3 to 2.2 in loss looks small; the matching drop in perplexity, from about 10 to 9, is easier to feel.

Comparing Values Carefully

Perplexity is only comparable when the setup matches:

  • Same tokenizer: a tokenizer with larger tokens has fewer, harder predictions, which changes per-token perplexity.
  • Same evaluation text: perplexity on news is not comparable with perplexity on code.
  • Same context length: models given more preceding context predict better.

Normalising by characters or bytes instead of tokens, giving bits per byte, allows fairer comparisons across tokenizers.

What It Doesn't Tell You

Low perplexity means a model predicts text well. It does not directly measure whether the model follows instructions, reasons correctly or gives safe answers. Preference tuning often makes a model more useful while slightly increasing its perplexity on raw text, because it shifts the model away from imitating the average document.

It remains valuable during pre-training, where it tracks learning progress smoothly and predictably, which is exactly what scaling laws are built on.

Closing Thought

Perplexity answers one narrow question very precisely: how surprised is this model by this text? That is the foundation of everything else, but not a substitute for measuring the rest.
