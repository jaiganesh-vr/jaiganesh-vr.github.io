Byte-Pair Encoding: How Text Becomes Tokens

Algorithms NLP

Jan 15, 2026

Language models do not read words or characters. They read tokens: chunks of text drawn from a fixed vocabulary. The algorithm that builds most of those vocabularies is byte-pair encoding, a technique that started life as a data compression method.

Understanding it explains a surprising number of model quirks.

The Algorithm

Training a BPE tokenizer is a simple greedy loop:

  • Start with a vocabulary of individual bytes or characters.
  • Count every adjacent pair of symbols in the training corpus.
  • Merge the most frequent pair into a new symbol and add it to the vocabulary.
  • Repeat until the vocabulary reaches the target size.

Early merges capture common pairs like "th" or "in". Later merges build whole words like "the" and frequent fragments like "ing". The final vocabulary is a mix of full words, subwords and single bytes.

Encoding New Text

To tokenize new text, the learned merges are replayed in the order they were learned. Common words collapse into a single token. Rare words are broken into familiar pieces. Nothing is ever unknown, because in the worst case text falls back to individual bytes.

Byte-level BPE guarantees that any string, in any language, can be encoded.

Why Vocabulary Size Matters

Vocabularies typically range from tens of thousands to a few hundred thousand tokens. A larger vocabulary means shorter sequences, which saves compute and context space, but it also means a larger embedding table and more rarely-seen tokens.

In English, one token averages roughly four characters, or about three quarters of a word. Other languages are often split into more tokens per word, which makes them more expensive to process and leaves less room in the same context window.

Quirks Explained by Tokens

Several well-known model weaknesses trace back to tokenization:

  • Counting letters: a word like "strawberry" may be two or three tokens, so the model never sees its individual letters directly.
  • Arithmetic: numbers are split inconsistently, so digits do not always line up by place value.
  • Whitespace and code: leading spaces are often part of tokens, so " the" and "the" are different symbols.

Closing Thought

Tokenization is easy to dismiss as preprocessing, but it defines the alphabet a model thinks in. Many behaviors that look like reasoning failures are really the model working with pieces it was never shown separately.
