Top-k and Top-p Sampling

Algorithms Decoding

Mar 05, 2026

Sampling from a language model's full distribution produces natural, varied text most of the time. Occasionally, though, it picks a token from the long tail of unlikely options, and one bad token can derail everything that follows.

Top-k and top-p are two simple algorithms that cut off that tail before sampling.

Top-k Sampling

Top-k keeps only the k most likely tokens, sets every other probability to zero, renormalises, and samples from what remains.

  • With k = 1, it is greedy decoding.
  • With k = 40, the model chooses among its forty best candidates.

The weakness is that k is fixed. When the model is confident, forty candidates is far too many and lets in junk. When the model is genuinely uncertain, such as at the start of a creative sentence, forty may be too few.

Top-p (Nucleus) Sampling

Top-p adapts to the shape of the distribution. It sorts tokens by probability and keeps the smallest set whose cumulative probability reaches p, for example 0.9, then samples from that set.

  • When one token has 95 percent of the mass, the nucleus is a single token.
  • When probability is spread evenly, the nucleus may contain hundreds.

Top-p asks how many options are reasonable here, instead of assuming a fixed number.

The Algorithm

  • Apply temperature to the logits and compute softmax.
  • Sort tokens by probability, descending.
  • Accumulate probabilities until the running total reaches p.
  • Discard everything after that point, renormalise and sample.

Sorting a vocabulary of over a hundred thousand tokens every step sounds expensive, but on modern hardware it is small next to the model's forward pass. Implementations often apply top-k first to shrink the candidate set before top-p.

Interaction With Temperature

Temperature changes the shape of the distribution; top-p then decides how much of it to keep. A higher temperature flattens probabilities, which makes the nucleus larger. That is why the two are usually tuned together, and why changing both at once can be hard to reason about.

A practical approach is to fix top-p around 0.9 to 0.95 and adjust only temperature for the task.

Other Variants

Min-p keeps tokens whose probability is at least a fraction of the top token's probability, which scales the cutoff with the model's confidence. It follows the same principle: trust the head of the distribution, distrust the tail.

Closing Thought

These algorithms are tiny compared to the models they serve, but they have an outsized effect on how output feels. Most of the difference between coherent and erratic sampling lives in where the tail gets cut.
