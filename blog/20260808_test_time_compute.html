Reasoning Models and Test-Time Compute

Machine Learning Reasoning

Aug 08, 2026

For years, the main way to make a model smarter was to make it larger and train it on more data. A newer lever has become just as important: letting a model spend more computation on each question at the time it is asked.

The Idea

Humans do not answer every question with the same effort. A greeting gets an instant reply; a proof gets an hour of scratch work. Test-time compute gives models a similar ability to think longer when a problem is hard.

Some capability can be bought at inference time instead of training time.

Early Forms

  • Chain-of-thought prompting: asking a model to reason step by step before answering improves accuracy on multi-step problems.
  • Self-consistency: sampling many reasoning paths and taking the majority answer is more reliable than any single path.
  • Best-of-n with a verifier: generating many candidates and choosing the one a checker scores highest.

These showed that extra inference effort reliably converts into accuracy.

Reasoning Models

Reasoning models build this into training. They are trained, largely with reinforcement learning, to produce long internal reasoning before a final answer. Rewards often come from verifiable outcomes, such as a maths answer matching the reference or code passing tests.

Through that training, models learn behaviors that were never explicitly programmed: checking their work, backtracking from dead ends and trying alternative approaches.

Performance on hard maths, science and coding problems improves steadily as models are allowed to reason longer.

The Trade-offs

  • Cost and latency: thinking tokens are generated and billed like any other output, and they take time.
  • Diminishing returns: easy questions gain nothing from long reasoning, and some tasks see little benefit at all.
  • Overthinking: models can talk themselves out of correct answers or pad simple tasks.

Many systems now let users or developers set a reasoning budget, or let the model decide how much to think based on difficulty.

Why It Matters

Test-time compute changes scaling from a single axis to two. Instead of only asking how big a model is, you can ask how much effort it should spend on this particular request.

Closing Thought

The shift to reasoning models is less about new architecture and more about giving models time. Many problems that looked out of reach turned out to need a model that was allowed to think before it spoke.
