Greedy, Beam Search and Sampling

Algorithms Decoding

Feb 10, 2026

A language model does not generate text directly. At every step it produces a probability distribution over possible next tokens. A decoding algorithm decides which token to actually pick, then feeds it back in and repeats.

The same model can feel sharp or repetitive depending on that choice.

Greedy Decoding

The simplest strategy picks the single most likely token at every step. It is deterministic, fast and easy to reason about.

Its weakness is that it is short-sighted. The locally best token is not always part of the best overall sequence, and greedy decoding is prone to loops, where a phrase becomes likely because it was just said.

Beam Search

Beam search keeps the k most likely partial sequences, called beams, at each step. Every beam is extended by its most likely next tokens, and only the top k overall survive.

  • It finds sequences with higher total probability than greedy decoding.
  • It works well for tasks with one correct output, like translation or speech recognition.
  • Its cost grows with the beam width, since k sequences are tracked in parallel.

For open-ended generation, beam search has a known flaw: the most probable text is often bland and repetitive. Human writing is rarely the single most likely continuation.

Maximising probability and maximising quality are not the same goal.

Sampling

Sampling draws the next token at random according to the model's distribution. This produces varied, natural-sounding text, but pure sampling occasionally picks a very unlikely token and derails.

In practice, sampling is almost always combined with controls: temperature to sharpen or flatten the distribution, and top-k or top-p truncation to remove the unreliable tail.

Choosing a Strategy

  • Structured or factual output: greedy or low-temperature sampling.
  • Translation and summarisation with classic models: beam search.
  • Chat, writing and brainstorming: sampling with temperature and top-p.

Modern chat models are tuned with sampling in mind, which is why it is the default in most APIs.

Closing Thought

Decoding is where a probability distribution becomes a sentence. It is one of the few places where you can change model behavior meaningfully without retraining anything.
